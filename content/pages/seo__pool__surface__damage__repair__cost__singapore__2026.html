Common Types of Pool Surface Damage

  • Cracking — hairline or structural cracks caused by ground movement, age, or poor original application
  • Rough, sandpaper-like texture — usually from chemical imbalance or age-related surface erosion
  • Staining and discolouration — from metals in the water, algae, or poor water chemistry
  • Flaking or peeling finish — often seen in painted or older plaster-finished pools
  • Etching — surface pitting caused by consistently low pH or improper chemical dosing
  • Delamination — surface finish separating from the substrate, often linked to a failed waterproofing layer

What Causes Pool Surface Damage in Singapore?

  • Water chemistry imbalance — Singapore's warm climate accelerates chemical reactions, so pH and chlorine levels drift faster and need closer monitoring
  • UV and heat exposure — constant tropical sun breaks down surface coatings over time
  • Age — most pool surface finishes last 10–15 years before needing resurfacing
  • Structural movement — settling or shifting in the pool shell transfers stress to the surface finish
  • Poor original workmanship — insufficient curing time or incorrect material mix during original construction
  • Abrasive cleaning tools or harsh chemicals used during maintenance

Pool Surface Repair Solutions

1. Spot Patch Repair

For small cracks or localised damage, our technicians patch the affected area with matching material, restoring both appearance and watertightness — ideal for early-stage issues.

2. Chemical Treatment & Stain Removal

Surface staining and mild etching can often be corrected with professional-grade acid washing or stain treatment without full resurfacing, provided the underlying finish is still structurally sound.

3. Full Surface Resurfacing

When damage is widespread — extensive cracking, roughness, or delamination — the existing surface is stripped back and a new finish is applied. This is the most comprehensive and long-lasting solution.

4. Waterproofing Repair Beneath the Surface

If surface damage is linked to water intrusion from behind (delamination), we repair the waterproofing membrane before reapplying the surface finish — otherwise the damage will return.

How to Prevent Pool Surface Damage

  • Test and balance water chemistry regularly — aim for pH between 7.2–7.6
  • Avoid using metal tools or abrasive brushes on delicate surface finishes
  • Schedule regular professional pool maintenance to catch early wear
  • Address small cracks immediately before they expand under water pressure
  • Ensure proper water circulation to reduce chemical stagnation and staining

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a pool surface be resurfaced?

Most pool surface finishes last 10–15 years in Singapore's climate before resurfacing is needed, though water chemistry management can extend or shorten this significantly.

Can I resurface my pool myself?

DIY resurfacing is not recommended — it requires precise material mixing, curing conditions, and application technique. Poor DIY work often fails within a year and can trap water behind the surface, worsening damage.

Why does my pool surface feel rough?

A rough surface usually indicates chemical erosion (often from low pH) or age-related wear on the finish. It's typically a sign resurfacing or professional treatment is needed soon.

How long does pool resurfacing take?

A full resurfacing project typically takes 5–10 days including draining, surface preparation, application, and curing time before refilling.

Is pool surface damage linked to water loss?

Yes — cracked or delaminated surfaces can allow water to seep into the structure. If you're also seeing your water level drop, read our pool water level dropping guide.
