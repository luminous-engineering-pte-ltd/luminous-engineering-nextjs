A pool leak in Singapore rarely announces itself with a dramatic burst pipe. It shows up quietly — a water level that drops faster than the weather explains, a spike on your utility bill, or a patch of soggy grass beside the pool deck. Left alone, even a small leak can waste thousands of litres of water a month, damage the pool structure, and undermine paving or foundations nearby. This guide walks you through the exact signs of a pool leak, what typically causes it in Singapore's climate, how professionals detect and fix it, and what pool leak repair actually costs in 2026 — so you know what to expect before you call a contractor.

Is It a Leak or Just Evaporation?

Not every drop in water level is a leak. In Singapore's heat, an uncovered pool can lose roughly 3mm to 5mm of water per day to evaporation alone, more on hot, windy, low-humidity days. A genuine leak is suspected when the water loss is faster than that, happens even when the pool isn't in use, or continues at the same rate whether the pump is on or off (which points to a leak in the shell or plumbing rather than the filtration equipment).

7 Warning Signs of a Pool Leak

  • Water level drops noticeably within 24 hours, even with the pump switched off.
  • Unexplained rise in your utility or water bill for two consecutive months.
  • Cracks appearing in pool tiles, coping, or the surrounding deck.
  • Damp or soggy patches, mould, or unusually lush grass near the pool equipment area.
  • Air bubbling from the return jets or a pump that keeps losing prime.
  • A constantly running top-up valve or auto-fill system.
  • Difficulty keeping chlorine and pH balanced despite regular dosing.

What Causes Pool Leaks in Singapore's Climate

Singapore's combination of intense heat, high humidity, and over 2,300mm of annual rainfall is tough on pool structures. The most common causes we see on-site include:

  • Cracked or perished pipe fittings, unions, and valves in the plant room.
  • Underground pipe leaks caused by ground movement or soil settlement after heavy rain.
  • Hairline cracks in the pool shell from thermal expansion and contraction.
  • Failed or aged waterproofing membrane beneath the tiles, allowing water to seep through the structure.
  • Worn pump shaft seals, O-rings, or gaskets at the filtration equipment.
  • Loose or deteriorated skimmer and light niche fittings.

DIY Bucket Test Before You Call a Contractor

Place a bucket filled with pool water on the pool step so the water levels inside and outside the bucket match. Mark both levels and check again after 24 hours. If the pool water has dropped noticeably more than the bucket water, you likely have a genuine leak rather than evaporation — it's time to bring in a professional for accurate leak detection.

Professional Leak Detection & Repair Solutions

A qualified pool contractor uses dye testing, pressure testing, and electronic leak detection equipment to pinpoint the exact source before any repair work begins — this avoids the guesswork (and wasted cost) of patching the wrong area. Depending on where the leak is found, the fix may involve:

  • Resealing or replacing fittings, unions, and valves at the plant room.
  • Epoxy injection or structural patching for shell cracks.
  • Re-routing or repairing underground pipework.
  • Reapplying the waterproofing membrane if the leak originates from a failed waterproofing layer.
  • Replacing pump seals, O-rings, and gaskets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How much does pool leak repair cost in Singapore?

A: Most equipment-side leak repairs cost SGD 150–800, while underground pipe or structural repairs typically run SGD 1,000–5,000. Severe waterproofing-related leaks can cost SGD 10,000 or more if a full membrane replacement is needed.

Q: How do I know if my pool is leaking or just evaporating?

A: Run the bucket test: if the pool loses noticeably more water than a bucket sitting on the pool step over 24 hours, and the loss continues with the pump off, it's likely a leak, not evaporation.

Q: Can a small pool leak be left alone?

A: No — even a minor leak wastes water, drives up your utility bill, and can worsen into structural or waterproofing damage over time, which costs significantly more to repair later.

Q: How long does pool leak detection take?

A: A standard dye or pressure test visit takes 1–3 hours. Underground leaks needing electronic tracing may take longer, and a written report is provided after the visit.

Q: Do I need to drain my pool to fix a leak?

A: Not always. Equipment-side and many shell leaks can be repaired without full draining. Draining is usually only needed for underground pipe work or waterproofing membrane replacement.
