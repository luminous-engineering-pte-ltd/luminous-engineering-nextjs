Pool Pump Repair & Installation

Common Swimming Pool Problems We Repair

If you recognise one of these symptoms, here's what it may indicate and what we do about it. This is general guidance only — a site inspection is needed to confirm the exact cause for your pool.

My swimming pool pump is not working

Possible causes range from electrical faults and tripped circuits to a seized motor or failed capacitor. We test the motor and electrical components on site to confirm the fault before recommending a repair or replacement.

My swimming pool is losing water

Water loss can come from evaporation (normal in Singapore's climate), a leak in the pipework, a faulty valve, or a crack around fittings. We compare water-loss patterns and inspect likely leak points to narrow down the cause.

My pool has poor water circulation

Weak circulation is often linked to a struggling pump, a partially blocked impeller, an air leak in the suction line, or a clogged filter restricting flow. We check the full circulation path rather than assuming it's the pump alone.

My pool filter is not cleaning properly

This is usually due to worn or clogged filter media, a damaged cartridge, or an issue with the backwash cycle on sand filters. We inspect the filter internals to confirm whether a clean, a part replacement, or a full filter replacement is needed.

My pool pump is making strange noises

Grinding, screeching or loud humming typically points to worn bearings, a failing capacitor, or debris caught in the impeller. These issues tend to worsen quickly, so we recommend having them checked before the motor fails completely.

My pool equipment keeps shutting down

Repeated shutdowns can be caused by overheating, electrical faults, or a safety cut-out responding to a genuine mechanical problem. We check both the electrical supply and the equipment itself rather than simply resetting the system.

My pool water remains cloudy despite cleaning

Persistent cloudiness after cleaning often points to a filtration issue rather than a chemical one — a struggling pump, an undersized or failing filter, or inadequate circulation time. We assess the mechanical side alongside advising on chemical balance.

My pool pipes are leaking

Pipe leaks can occur at joints, valves, or along a damaged section of pipe, often due to age, ground movement, or pressure issues. We identify the affected section and repair or replace it using pressure-rated fittings.

My pool heater is not heating properly

This may be an ignition fault, a faulty thermostat, thermocouple wear, or a heat exchanger issue on older units. We diagnose the specific fault before recommending repair or, where appropriate, replacement.

My chlorinator is not producing enough chlorine

Reduced chlorine output is commonly linked to a fouled or worn chlorinator cell, salt level issues, or a control board fault. We inspect the cell and controls to determine whether cleaning, a part replacement, or a new unit is the right solution.

Should You Repair or Replace Your Pool Equipment?

There's no single right answer — it depends on the equipment's age, the type of fault, and how it fits your budget. Here's a balanced way to think about it:

Repair is often the more practical choice when:

  • The equipment is relatively young and has otherwise been reliable
  • The fault involves a specific, replaceable component (seal, capacitor, thermocouple) rather than the core motor or heat exchanger
  • The repair cost is clearly a fraction of a full replacement
  • Compatible parts are readily available

Replacement becomes more sensible when:

  • The equipment is old and has needed frequent repairs
  • A major component — such as the motor or heat exchanger — has failed
  • Parts are difficult or impossible to source for an older model
  • A newer, more energy-efficient model would meaningfully reduce running costs over time

Our technicians give you a straightforward assessment of both options during inspection, so the decision is based on your equipment's actual condition rather than a one-size-fits-all recommendation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does swimming pool repair include?

Swimming pool repair covers diagnosing and fixing faults in pool equipment and plumbing — pumps, filters, heaters, chlorinators, lighting, pipes and valves — as well as identifying and repairing leaks. It typically starts with an inspection to confirm the cause before any repair work begins.

What are the signs that a swimming pool needs repair?

Common signs include reduced water flow, unusual noises from the pump, cloudy water despite regular cleaning, visible leaks around equipment, frequent circuit breaker trips, and noticeably higher energy bills. Any of these warrant an inspection.

How do I know if my pool pump needs repair?

If the pump won't start, makes grinding or screeching noises, runs but delivers weak circulation, or trips the circuit repeatedly, it likely needs inspection. A technician can confirm whether it's a minor component fault or a more serious motor issue.

Can a swimming pool leak be repaired?

In most cases, yes — but the right fix depends on the source and severity of the leak. Leaks at fittings, valves, or pipe joints are generally straightforward to repair, while leaks tied to structural or waterproofing issues may need a different specialist. An inspection identifies which applies.

What is included in swimming pool equipment installation?

Equipment installation covers correctly sizing and fitting new pumps, filters, heaters, chlorinators, or lighting, ensuring compatibility with your existing system, and testing everything under normal operating conditions before handover.

Should I repair or replace my swimming pool equipment?

It depends on the equipment's age, the type of fault, and repair cost relative to replacement cost. Younger equipment with a minor, specific fault is usually worth repairing; older equipment with major component failure or frequent repeat issues often makes more sense to replace. See the Repair vs Replace section above for a fuller breakdown.
