Common Pool Pump Problems in Singapore

1. Pool Pump Not Turning On

Usually caused by a tripped circuit breaker, faulty capacitor, worn-out motor bearings, or a seized impeller. In older HDB and landed properties, ageing wiring can also be the culprit.

2. Pool Pump Running But No Water Flow

This typically points to a clogged skimmer basket or pump strainer, a blocked impeller, an air leak on the suction side, or a closed/faulty valve somewhere in the plumbing line.

3. Pool Pump Making Loud or Grinding Noises

Grinding or screeching sounds usually mean worn bearings inside the motor. Rattling can indicate debris caught in the impeller, while a high-pitched whine often signals the motor is starving for water (a sign of a suction blockage).

4. Pool Pump Leaking Water

Leaks commonly come from a worn mechanical seal, cracked pump housing, deteriorated O-rings, or loose union fittings. Left unresolved, this accelerates motor corrosion and can trip your electrical supply.

5. Pool Pump Overheating or Tripping the Breaker

Overheating is often caused by restricted airflow, a failing capacitor, electrical faults, or the pump running dry due to low water level or a blocked suction line.

6. Reduced Water Circulation / Weak Suction

A gradual drop in performance is frequently linked to impeller wear, a partially clogged filter, or an ageing pump that's simply reaching the end of its 8–10 year service life.

Quick DIY Checks Before Calling a Technician

  • Check the circuit breaker and power supply first — sometimes it really is that simple
  • Clean out the skimmer basket and pump strainer basket of leaves and debris
  • Inspect for visible cracks, leaks, or loose fittings around the pump housing
  • Check the pool water level — it should sit at least halfway up the skimmer opening
  • Listen for unusual sounds and note when they occur (at startup vs continuously)

If the problem persists after these checks, it's time to bring in a certified technician — working with electrical components and pressurised plumbing without training carries real safety risk.

Repair or Replace? How to Decide

As a general rule:

  • Repair if the pump is under 5–7 years old, the fault is a minor component (seal, capacitor, O-ring), and repair cost is below 50% of a new unit.
  • Replace if the pump is over 8–10 years old, the motor itself has failed, repairs have been frequent, or you want to switch to an energy-efficient variable-speed model that lowers long-term electricity bills.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my pool pump running but the water isn't clean?

This usually means the pump is circulating water but the filter isn't removing debris effectively — check for a clogged filter, insufficient run time, or an undersized pump for your pool volume.

How long should a pool pump run each day in Singapore?

Most residential pools need 6–8 hours of circulation daily, though this varies with pool size, bather load, and Singapore's warm climate, which accelerates algae growth.

How much does it cost to replace a pool pump in Singapore?

A standard replacement typically costs \$500–\$1,200 including labour, while energy-efficient variable-speed pumps start from around \$500–\$1,800+ depending on capacity and brand.

How often should a pool pump be serviced?

We recommend a professional inspection every 6–12 months, alongside routine cleaning of the strainer basket, to catch early signs of wear before they become costly repairs.

Can a faulty pool pump cause my water level to drop?

Yes — a leaking seal or cracked pump housing can slowly lose water, which is often mistaken for evaporation. If you're also noticing water loss, read our pool water level dropping guide.
