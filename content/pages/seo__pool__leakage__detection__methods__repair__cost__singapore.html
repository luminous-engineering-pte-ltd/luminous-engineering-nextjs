Signs of a Pool Leak

  • Water level dropping faster than normal evaporation (see our pool water level dropping guide for a full test method)
  • Cracks appearing in the pool deck, tiles, or surrounding structure
  • Unusually wet or soggy ground near the pool
  • A noticeable increase in water and chemical costs
  • Air bubbles in the pool return jets, suggesting a suction-side leak
  • Cracked or loose tiles at or below the waterline

Professional Pool Leak Detection Methods

1. Visual Inspection

Our technicians start with a thorough visual check of tiles, grout lines, skimmer and return fittings, equipment pad, and surrounding deck for obvious cracks, gaps, or damp areas.

2. Pressure Testing

Plumbing lines (suction and return) are isolated and pressurised with air or water to identify which line is losing pressure — pinpointing leaks in underground pipework without unnecessary digging.

3. Dye Testing

A small amount of coloured dye is released near suspected leak points — cracks, fittings, or light niches. If a leak is present, the dye is visibly drawn into the gap, confirming the exact location.

4. Acoustic Leak Detection

Specialised listening equipment detects the sound of water escaping through underground pipes, allowing technicians to trace leaks along the plumbing route with minimal disruption.

5. Bucket Test (Preliminary Check)

Before booking a professional inspection, homeowners can run a simple bucket test to confirm whether water loss exceeds normal evaporation rates — a useful first step covered in our pool water level guide.

Common Sources of Pool Leaks in Singapore

  • Cracked or delaminated tiles — often linked to a failed waterproofing membrane underneath
  • Skimmer and return fitting leaks — a very common leak point at the pool wall penetration
  • Underground plumbing leaks — cracked or corroded pipes connecting the pool to the pump and filter
  • Structural shell cracks — from ground settlement, age, or original construction issues
  • Light niche seal failure — the fitting around underwater lights is a frequent, easily overlooked leak source
  • Equipment pad leaks — pump seals, filter housings, and valve unions
