Is It Evaporation or a Leak? The Bucket Test

Before assuming the worst, run this simple test:

  1. Fill a bucket with pool water and place it on a pool step so the water levels inside and outside the bucket match.
  1. Mark the water level on both the bucket and the pool edge.
  1. Turn off the pump for accuracy (or note whether it's running), and wait 24 hours.
  1. Compare the two levels. If the pool has dropped significantly more than the bucket, you likely have a leak — not just evaporation.

In Singapore's hot, humid climate, normal evaporation is typically around 5–8mm per day depending on sun exposure and wind. Losses beyond this — especially with the pump off — point to a structural or plumbing leak.

Common Causes of Pool Water Level Dropping

  • Cracked or damaged tiles allowing water to seep behind the pool shell — see our guide on pool tile damage repair for related signs
  • Failed waterproofing membrane, especially in pools over 10 years old
  • Skimmer or return jet leaks, often at the fitting where it meets the pool wall
  • Cracked or corroded underground plumbing carrying water to and from the pump and filter
  • Pump seal or equipment pad leaks, which can look like pool water loss but originate at the equipment
  • Structural cracks in the pool shell, typically from ground movement or age
  • Normal evaporation, accelerated by heat, wind, and heavy pool usage — not a fault, but often mistaken for a leak

How to Narrow Down Where the Leak Is

  • Check equipment first — inspect the pump, filter, and visible plumbing for wet patches or drips
  • Look at the skimmer and return fittings — cracks around these fixtures are one of the most common leak points
  • Check the surrounding deck and soil — unusually soft or wet ground near the pool can indicate an underground leak
  • Note water loss with the pump on vs off — if the pool only loses water when the pump is running, the leak is likely in the plumbing lines under pressure

Why You Shouldn't Ignore a Dropping Water Level

  • Rising water bills from constantly topping up the pool
  • Chemical imbalance as fresh water dilutes your treatment, requiring more frequent dosing
  • Equipment damage — pumps running with insufficient water can overheat and fail prematurely
  • Structural risk — undetected leaks can erode soil beneath the pool deck or foundation over time
  • Escalating repair costs — a small skimmer leak today can become a major structural repair if left unresolved for years

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water loss is normal for a pool in Singapore?

Roughly 5–8mm per day is typical evaporation in our climate. Anything significantly beyond that, especially with the pump off, suggests a leak rather than normal evaporation.

Can a small leak fix itself?

No — leaks typically worsen over time as water pressure continues to erode the crack, gap, or seal. Early repair is always cheaper than delayed repair.

Will my water bill go up if my pool has a leak?

Yes, frequent topping up increases water usage significantly, and you'll also spend more on pool chemicals to rebalance diluted water.

How do professionals find a hidden pool leak?

We use a combination of pressure testing on plumbing lines, dye testing at suspected fittings, and acoustic listening equipment to pinpoint leaks without unnecessary excavation.

Is it safe to keep using my pool if it's losing water?

Yes, in most cases, but it's best to get the cause diagnosed quickly — continued water loss can affect chemical balance and, in structural cases, worsen the underlying damage.
