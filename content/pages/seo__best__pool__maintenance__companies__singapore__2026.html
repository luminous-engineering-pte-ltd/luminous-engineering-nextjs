What's Actually Included in Pool Maintenance

A proper maintenance visit is more than someone scooping leaves out with a net. At minimum, it should include skimming debris off the surface, brushing the walls and floor to stop algae getting a foothold, vacuuming any sediment that's settled, and testing the water for pH, chlorine, and alkalinity — then adjusting chemicals based on what those readings show, not on a fixed schedule regardless of conditions.

Beyond the weekly visit, a good company also checks the equipment room briefly each time: is the pump running quietly and at the right pressure, is the filter due for a backwash or clean, does the water level need topping up. Catching a struggling pump early is far cheaper than replacing one that's burnt out from months of unnoticed strain. Some companies bundle this equipment check into standard visits; others charge it separately, so it's worth asking directly rather than assuming.

For landed properties and older pools specifically, tile and grout condition is worth asking about too — a small tile crack or grout gap can let water seep behind the pool shell, which is a very different (and more expensive) problem than routine cleaning.

How to Choose a Pool Maintenance Company

1. Match the package to how often you actually swim. A pool used daily needs weekly (sometimes twice-weekly) visits in Singapore's climate; a rarely-used pool can often get by on bi-weekly or monthly, saving on cost without risking a green pool.

2. Ask what "maintenance" actually includes. Some packages are cleaning-only; others include a quick equipment check every visit. Confirm this upfront so you're not surprised by extra charges when the pump needs attention.

3. Check if they handle repairs, or only cleaning. If your pool has an older pump, ageing tiles, or a history of leaks, a company that can also repair — not just clean — saves you from coordinating two separate contractors.

4. Ask about water testing specifics. A company that tests and logs pH, chlorine, and alkalinity every visit (rather than eyeballing the water) is more likely to catch problems before they turn into algae blooms or equipment strain.

5. Get pricing in writing before the first visit. Chemical costs can vary visit to visit depending on how much dosing is needed — ask whether this is included in the package price or billed separately.

6. Look for consistency in reviews, not just star ratings. A handful of reviews mentioning the same technician by name, or consistently praising clear communication, is often a better signal than an average star rating alone.

FAQs

How often does a residential pool in Singapore actually need cleaning? For a pool that's used regularly, weekly visits are the standard recommendation given the local climate — heat and rain both accelerate algae growth and chemical drift. Bi-weekly or monthly can work for pools used less often, but expect more work (and cost) if the pool has been neglected between visits.

Why does my pool keep turning green even with regular cleaning? This is almost always a chemical balance issue rather than a cleaning frequency issue — low chlorine, poor pH, or a struggling filtration system let algae take hold even with regular skimming. A company that tests water chemistry properly each visit should catch this before it becomes visible.

Is it worth repairing an old pool or should I consider rebuilding? This depends on the extent of the issue — a single tile crack or grout gap is usually a straightforward repair, but recurring leaks, structural cracking, or ageing plumbing throughout can make a partial or full rebuild more cost-effective long-term. A company that handles both maintenance and construction (rather than cleaning only) can usually give you an honest read on which makes more sense.

Do pool maintenance companies also handle Jacuzzis and water features? Many do, though not all — it's worth confirming this specifically if you have a Jacuzzi, spa, or pond alongside your main pool, since some companies focus purely on the pool itself.
