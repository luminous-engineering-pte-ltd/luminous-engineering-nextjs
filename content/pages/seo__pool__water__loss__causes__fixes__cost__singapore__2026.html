“Why is my pool losing so much water?” is one of the most common questions Singapore pool owners ask. Some water loss is completely normal in our tropical heat. But when the water level keeps dropping day after day, faster than the weather can explain, you're likely paying for a leak somewhere in the system — and possibly for structural damage that's getting worse the longer it's ignored. Here's how to tell the difference, what usually causes real water loss in Singapore, and what it costs to fix.

Normal Water Loss vs a Real Problem

Evaporation in Singapore's climate typically accounts for 3mm to 5mm of water loss per day, higher during hot, dry, or windy spells, and higher still for heated or exposed pools. If your pool is losing more than about 6mm a day, or losing water even when it's covered and unused, evaporation is unlikely to be the full story.

Common Causes of Pool Water Loss in Singapore

  • Leaking pipe joints, unions, or valves in the plant room.
  • Worn pump shaft seals or a failing pump causing water to seep out during operation.
  • Cracks in the pool shell, especially around steps, the skimmer box, and light niches.
  • Damaged or deteriorated waterproofing membrane beneath the tiles.
  • Splash-out from overfilling, heavy usage, or rain overflow through the overflow drain.
  • Underground pipe leaks caused by soil movement after Singapore's heavier monsoon rains.

How to Test for Real Water Loss (The Bucket Test)

Fill a bucket with pool water and place it on a step so the inside and outside water levels match. Mark both, wait 24 hours, and compare. If the pool has dropped noticeably more than the bucket, you have a leak rather than ordinary evaporation. Also check whether the water loss continues with the pump switched off — if it does, the leak is likely in the shell or underground plumbing rather than the filtration system.

How Professionals Fix Pool Water Loss

Once a leak is confirmed, a qualified technician isolates the source using pressure testing, dye testing, and visual inspection of the plant room, skimmer, and shell before repairing it. Typical fixes include:

  • Resealing plant room fittings and valves.
  • Replacing pump seals and gaskets.
  • Epoxy injection for shell and step cracks.
  • Repairing or re-routing underground pipework.
  • Reapplying waterproofing membrane where the loss is traced to a failed membrane.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How much water loss is normal for a pool in Singapore?

A: Around 3mm to 5mm per day from evaporation is normal in Singapore's climate. Anything consistently higher, especially with the pump off, usually indicates a leak.

Q: Why does my pool lose water only when the pump is running?

A: This usually points to a leak on the pressure side of the plumbing, such as a return line, jet fitting, or a fault at the pool light niche, rather than the shell itself.

Q: Can heavy rain cause pool water loss?

A: Rain itself adds water, but heavy monsoon rainfall can shift soil around underground pipework, which over time can crack joints and cause a genuine leak.

Q: Is pool water loss covered by home insurance in Singapore?

A: This depends on your policy and whether the loss is due to gradual wear (usually excluded) or sudden accidental damage. Check your policy terms or ask your insurer directly.

Q: How fast should I fix a water loss problem?

A: As soon as a leak is confirmed. Continued water loss increases utility costs and can escalate a simple fitting leak into a costlier structural or waterproofing repair.
