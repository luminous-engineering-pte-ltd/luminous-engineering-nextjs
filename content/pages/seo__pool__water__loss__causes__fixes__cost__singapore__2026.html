Normal Water Loss vs a Real Problem

Singapore pools can lose water through evaporation and splash-out, but a level that drops quickly every day, keeps falling when the pool is not used, or leaves wet areas nearby should be treated as a possible leak.

Common Causes of Pool Water Loss

  • Evaporation during hot, windy periods.
  • Splash-out from heavy use.
  • Backwashing or drainage valves left open.
  • Leaking pump seals, unions or filter equipment.
  • Cracked pipes, fittings, tiles or pool structure.
  • Failed waterproofing behind the finish.

How to Check With the Bucket Test

Place a bucket of pool water on a step, mark both water levels and compare after 24 hours. If the pool drops more than the bucket, investigate for leakage.

Fixes for Pool Water Loss

  • Adjust operation practices if the cause is evaporation, splash-out or over-backwashing.
  • Repair or replace worn pump seals, valves and equipment fittings.
  • Pressure-test pipework when underground leakage is suspected.
  • Repair tiles, cracks, grout or waterproofing when the pool shell is affected.

FAQs

How much pool water loss is normal in Singapore?

Some water loss is normal, especially outdoors. A sudden or persistent drop is not normal and should be tested.

Can a pool lose water without a visible leak?

Yes. Underground pipes, hidden fittings and waterproofing defects may leak without obvious surface signs.

Should I switch off the pump?

If the pump is running dry, noisy or losing prime, stop it and arrange inspection to avoid equipment damage.
